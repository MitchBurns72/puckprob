Quality Control

Every PuckProb model is replayed on past seasons, at the same date, with only what was known that day, then compared with what really happened. It gets the PuckProb seal only when its % match reality. If not, we say so, and we fix it.

Gameday Probs: the detail

Are our percentages right? Every prediction is checked after the game. If we say 70% a hundred times, it should happen about 70 times: the “It happened” column shows it, bucket by bucket.

+8.8%better than guessing
15,244predictions checked

Our probabilities against the “know-nothing” guess (always saying the average rate). Measured with the Brier score, the standard yardstick for probabilities: it rewards being right and being confident only when it’s deserved.

Score /100: 50 = no better than giving everyone the same %, 100 = perfect (impossible). For players, 55 to 60 is a good model; for wins, even the best public models sit around 52 to 55 — hockey is very random. Right side: how often the side we favored actually happened. Better than guessing: how much smaller our probability error (Brier score) is than always saying the average rate. 0% = no better, positive = we beat it.

Nights counted: 29 predicted before the games, 18 replayed afterwards using only the data available before each game (to build history from day one).

Our in-house xG

100% = as good as MoneyPuck at predicting the goals players actually score (same players, same games, every season compared). Over 100% = better.

xG (expected goals) is the chance a shot becomes a goal, based on where and how it was taken. Ours is built from the NHL play-by-play, and every PuckProb model now runs on it. Like MoneyPuck: the correlation between their xG and ours, player by player (100% = identical), 2026-27 season. Goals ranked right: the chance that a shot that went in had a higher xG than one that didn't (50% = coin flip; good public models sit around 75 to 80%).
