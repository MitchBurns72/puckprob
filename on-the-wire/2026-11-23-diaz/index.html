Thank you, Raphael Diaz: how a Swiss defenseman turned into Dylan Larkin

Special edition · By PuckProb.ca · November 23, 2026

Everybody's talking about Larkin. I'm talking about the man who made it possible 12 years ago without knowing it: Raphael Diaz. Five trades, 12 years, and a red paperclip that ends up as a second-line center. Let's untangle it.

You know the story of the guy who traded a red paperclip and, swap after swap, ended up with a house? Well, the Habs just pulled off the hockey version. The paperclip is Raphael Diaz. The house is Dylan Larkin. And in between, 12 years of magic nobody saw coming. Not even Diaz. Especially not Diaz.

Patient zero

2011: the Habs sign an undrafted Swiss defenseman, Raphael Diaz, out of Zug. A nice player, a good first pass, nothing to get the Bell Centre on its feet. On February 3, 2014, he goes to Vancouver for Dale Weise, according to Eyes On The Prize. At the time, nobody popped the champagne. A third-pair defenseman for a fourth-liner. Turns out it was the steal of the decade and nobody knew it.

The whole chain

Hang on, this goes fast. According to A Winning Habit, which dug up the entire tree:

  • 2014: Diaz goes to Vancouver for Dale Weise.
  • 2016: Weise and Tomas Fleischmann go to Chicago for Phillip Danault and a 2018 second-round pick.
  • 2018: that second-rounder becomes Alexander Romanov.
  • 2022: Romanov and a fourth-round pick go to Long Island for an Islanders first-round pick.
  • 2022: that first-rounder (who became Frank Nazar) and a third-round pick go to Chicago for Kirby Dach.
  • 2026: Dach is part of the big package sent to Detroit for Larkin.

And along the way, Danault became the shutdown center on the Habs team that reached the 2021 Stanley Cup Final. He's even back in Montreal now. The paperclip bore fruit on the way, like an apple tree growing in your yard that you never planted.

Let's be honest, though

I won't sugarcoat it: Larkin didn't cost just Dach. The Habs also gave up Samuel Montembeault, prospect Michael Hage and four picks, including two firsts. Diaz paid the cover charge, not the whole bill. But he got a foot in the door. No Diaz, no Weise. No Weise, no Danault or Romanov. No Romanov, no Dach. And without Dach, Detroit would have wanted even more. It's the butterfly effect, Bell Centre edition: a flap of wings in Zug in 2011, and a hurricane in Detroit in 2026.

Meanwhile, Diaz

While his name became the root of the Habs' prettiest trade tree, Diaz went back to Switzerland, to Zug, where he was captain and a champion, according to his Wikipedia page. He probably has no idea that on a Sunday night in November, he helped bring a former Red Wings captain to Montreal. Can somebody text him? Or send him a fondue, at least.

My verdict

This is the best story of the week, and I'm not budging: Kent Hughes, and Marc Bergevin before him, played chess over 12 years without ever talking to each other. No wild prediction here: Larkin still has a whole season to play in Montreal. But one thing's for sure: the next time the Habs trade a third-pair defenseman for a fourth-liner, I'm watching with a lot of respect.
