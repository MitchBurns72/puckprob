Larkin in Montreal: what really changes down the middle

By PuckProb.ca · November 23, 2026

The morning after: we went through the Habs' lineup with a fine-tooth comb. With Larkin, second-line center goes from a committee losing the chance battle to a 72-point-a-season guy. Suzuki gets relief, Danault and Evans get their real jobs back, and I'm making three predictions.

The dust is settling on Sunday night's trade. The Hockey Writers already handed out grades and RDS calls it a fan wish come true. But the real question is what Dylan Larkin actually changes in the Habs' lineup. We ran the numbers, spot by spot.

The hole at second-line center

Before Larkin, the Habs' second-line center was a committee. And the committee was losing the scoring-chance battle:

  • Alex Newhook: 12 points in 24 games, 16.4 minutes a night. On the ice, 17.8 PBxG for, 24.5 against.
  • Phillip Danault: 9 points in 24 games, 17.2 minutes. 17.2 PBxG for, 21.2 against.
  • Jake Evans: 10 points in 24 games. 13.6 PBxG for, 20.6 against.
  • Kirby Dach, now in Detroit: 8 points in 22 games, 10.6 for, 10.6 against. Break-even, at 11.7 minutes.

Larkin arrives with 137 points in 156 games over his last two seasons, a 72-point pace per 82 games. Even in Detroit's mess this fall, he played 21.7 minutes and the Wings controlled play with him on the ice: 11.1 PBxG for, 7.7 against. He wins 53% of his faceoffs. His PB Market Value is $9.0M on an $8.7M contract. The Habs are paying fair price, not a premium.

The domino effect

The best part is what it frees up below. Danault and Evans take 70% of the Habs' defensive-zone faceoffs between them, and they win them (56% and 55%). They're defensive specialists, and that's fine. The problem is they were also being asked to produce. My honest take: Danault, paid $5.5M, is giving what our PB Market Value rates at the league minimum ($0.85M). In a pure third-line role, that contract gets a lot easier to carry. Newhook, I see sliding to the wing or the third line. His PB Market Value ($3.8M) tops his contract ($2.9M): he has value, just not as a top-six center.

Suzuki can finally breathe

Nick Suzuki has been carrying the top line since day one: 20 points in 18 games, 20.8 minutes a night, 31.8 PBxG for against 20.8. He's 4th in our Selke race (4.7%) and his $14.0M PB Market Value is nearly double his contract ($7.9M). With Larkin soaking up the other tough matchup, opponents' best lines have to split in two. That should help Cole Caufield (12 goals, 9th in our Rocket Richard race) and, hopefully, Juraj Slafkovský, who has 2 goals in 24 games despite good chances. And on the back end, Lane Hutson (24 points, 6th in our Norris race) gets more people to feed.

My predictions

This morning, the Habs are 10th in our Power rankings, with 32 points in 24 games, projected at 102.5 points, a 79.5% chance of making the playoffs and 3.5% of winning the Cup. I'll go out on a limb: Larkin puts up 45 points in 58 games with Montreal, the Habs crack our top 8 before Christmas, and their playoff chances clear 85%. The big caveat: Larkin hasn't played since October 29. A month off rusts even a captain. Give him two weeks before judging.
