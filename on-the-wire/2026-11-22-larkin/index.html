Larkin heads to Montreal: the Habs empty the closet for a real second-line center

Special edition · By PuckProb.ca · November 22, 2026

The Larkin saga is over. Montreal sends Kirby Dach, Samuel Montembeault, prospect Michael Hage and four picks, including two conditional firsts, to Detroit for Dylan Larkin. Our numbers say the Habs pay a lot, but they buy exactly what they were missing. Plus: what's really wrong with Slafkovský, and Bedard on injured reserve.

Well, well. A Sunday night in November, and Kent Hughes swings the big bat. According to TSN and The Hockey News, Montreal gets Dylan Larkin, a 2028 second-round pick and a 2027 third-round pick. Detroit gets Kirby Dach, Samuel Montembeault, prospect Michael Hage, two first-round picks (2027 and 2029, both conditional), a 2027 second-rounder and a 2028 fourth-rounder. Seven pieces for one player. The conditions on the two firsts haven't been released yet.

What Montreal is buying

Forget the six points in 10 games this season: Larkin hasn't played since his October 29 suspension. The real Larkin looks like this in PB Stats:

  • 2023-24: 69 points in 68 games (33 goals).
  • 2024-25: 70 points in 82 games (30 goals).
  • 2025-26: 67 points in 74 games (34 goals).
  • 2026-27: 6 points in 10 games, at 21.7 minutes a night before he vanished.

Three straight seasons around 70 points. And even after nearly a month without playing, he's still 137th among players in our Power rankings. His PB Market Value is $9.0M on an $8.7M contract: the Habs are paying market price for a second-line center, not a panic premium. My blunt take: this is the player Montreal has needed ever since Nick Suzuki started carrying the top line by himself.

What Montreal is giving up

The price is huge, no point pretending otherwise. Two first-round picks and Michael Hage, Montreal's 2024 first-rounder that everyone saw as a piece of the future. That one stings.

The rest stings less. Kirby Dach has 8 points in 22 games, at 11.7 minutes a night. His PB Market Value is $3.1M on a $3.6M contract: he's paid more than he's giving back. Last season it was 15 points in 37 games. Montreal waited years for him to become the second-line center; tonight, it stopped waiting. As for Samuel Montembeault, our goalie numbers have no NHL games from him this season. In net, Jacob Fowler leads the way: 6th among goalies in our Power rankings, a PBGC of 65 and a .918 save percentage. Jakub Dobes (.864) has more work to do.

The Habs, before the move

Montreal has 32 points in 24 games. Our simulations project them at 102.5 points, with an 80% chance of making the playoffs and 3.4% of winning the Cup (10th favorite). They're 10th in our team Power rankings. And the kids are pushing: Lane Hutson is 32nd among players, Suzuki 49th. Money-wise, our numbers had Montreal $8.4M under the cap before the trade. Larkin comes in at $8.7M, Dach leaves with his $3.6M, and Montembeault's contract goes too. It fits.

No big prediction tonight: our playoff and Cup chances will be recalculated with Larkin in the lineup at the next update. But a 70-point second-line center behind Suzuki is the piece a team already at 80% was missing. The big question mark: nearly a month off rusts a guy, even a captain.

Detroit, on the other side

The Wings are 25th in our Power rankings, with 19 points in 23 games, a 7.4% chance of making the playoffs and 0.1% of winning the Cup. They have $24.9M in cap space by our numbers. Steve Yzerman had lost almost all of his leverage since the suspension. And he still walks away with two firsts, a top prospect, a 25-year-old center and a veteran goalie. Under the circumstances, that's a damn good return. The real test: the rest of their season with Lucas Raymond (36th in our Power rankings) as the new face of the team.

The verdict

Montreal pays a big price, but it pays for a sure thing: three straight seasons around 70 points. Detroit turns the page on a story that was poisoning its season. Nobody gets robbed, and both teams got what they wanted. For me, Montreal wins the trade today, and Detroit could win it in five years if Hage becomes what people think. That's exactly what a real blockbuster should be.

Slaf: what's wrong?

While we're on the Habs, let's talk about Juraj Slafkovský's struggles. Last season: 30 goals, 73 points in 82 games. This season: 2 goals, 14 points in 24 games. He's 170th in our Power rankings and his PB Market Value has slipped to $7.3M, under his $7.6M contract.

But dig one layer deeper and the picture changes. He still plays 18.4 minutes a night. He shoots about twice a game. Our PBxG give him around 0.4 expected goals per game, so 8 or 9 expected goals so far. He has 2. And when he's on the ice, Montreal generates 28.9 PBxG against 20.8 allowed. My verdict: his game isn't broken, the puck just won't go in. The chances are there, the finish will come back. What's wrong is the shooting, not the player.

Bedard on IR: no Bedarding for a while

In Chicago, Connor Bedard is on injured reserve, according to Pro Hockey Rumors. The Hawks recalled Dominic Toninato and, according to RDS, claimed Luke Glendening off waivers from the Rangers. We don't have a timeline, so I won't make one up.

For those who don't live on the forums: "Let's get Bedarded" (a play on the Black Eyed Peas' Let's Get It Started) is what fans yell when Bedard pulls off something crazy. Well, now it's the opposite: no Bedarding in Chicago for a while, and the whole city is in Let's Get It Stopped mode.

And the timing stinks. Bedard had 18 points in 15 games, and he's 37th among players in our Power rankings. His PB Market Value ($11.3M) is far from his $15.0M contract: Chicago is still paying for the potential. The Hawks are 32nd and last in our team Power rankings, with 16 points in 23 games and a 0.3% chance of making the playoffs. Without him, the bottom of the standings is theirs to lose.
